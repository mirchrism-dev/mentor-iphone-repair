iPad Won’t Turn On?

iPads tend to sit in a drawer or a kid’s backpack for weeks, so a black screen is often just a battery that drained all the way down. Other times it’s a charging port packed with lint, a worn-out battery, or a screen that’s working but not lighting up after a drop. Before you assume it’s gone for good, try the checks below. Common Reasons an iPad Stays Black

Completely drained battery

An iPad that’s been unused for a long time can need a while on the charger before it shows anything at all.

Charger, cable, or port problem

A damaged cable, a weak adapter, or debris in the Lightning or USB-C port can stop it from charging. See iPad charging port repair.

Worn-out battery

Older iPads may only turn on while plugged in, or shut off at 20–30%. See iPad battery replacement.

Display damage after a drop

The iPad may be on (it chimes, vibrates, or connects to a computer) while the screen stays dark. See iPad screen repair.

Safe Things to Try First

1

Charge it for at least an hour

Use a wall adapter, not a computer USB port, and a cable you know works on another device. Check back after an hour.

2

Try a force restart

On iPads with a Home button, hold the Home and top buttons together until the Apple logo appears. On iPads without one, press and release volume up, then volume down, then hold the top button until you see the logo.

3

Look at the charging port

Shine a light in. If you see packed lint, don’t dig with metal. We can clean it carefully.

4

Listen and feel

If it makes sounds or gets notifications but the screen is black, the problem is probably the display, not the whole iPad.

Got wet? Don’t charge it. See water damage help, which applies to tablets too.

iPad Won’t Turn On FAQs (Mentor, OH)

My iPad won’t turn on even when plugged in. Is it dead?

Not necessarily. A fully drained battery can take a while to show anything, and a bad cable or dirty port can stop it from charging at all. Try a different cable and wall adapter for an hour before deciding.

It only works while it’s plugged in. What does that mean?

That usually points to a worn-out battery. A battery replacement is often much cheaper than a new iPad.

My kid dropped it and now the screen is black but it still makes sounds.

The iPad is likely on and the display is damaged. A screen repair may get it working again. Bring it in and we’ll check.

Will I lose my photos and apps?

Diagnosing and most repairs don’t erase anything. If it’s been backed up to iCloud, that’s an extra safety net.
