A little warmth while charging, gaming, or using GPS in the car is normal. Stop Using It and Unplug If You Notice

A swollen or lifting screen

If the screen is pushing up or the back looks bulged, the battery may be swelling. Don’t charge it, don’t press on it, and bring it in.

A burning or chemical smell

Unplug right away and move the phone away from anything flammable. Don’t put it in the fridge or freezer.

Too hot to hold comfortably

Heat you can’t keep your hand on, especially when the phone is idle, isn’t normal use.

Heat near the charging port only

Heat focused at the bottom while charging can mean a damaged cable, a bad charger, or a problem in the port.

Things to Try First (When It’s Just Warm)

1

Cool it down the gentle way

Take off the case, get it out of the sun or the car, and let it rest. Avoid cold packs or the freezer; sudden cold can cause condensation inside.

2

Check Settings › Battery

Look at which apps use the most battery. An app stuck running in the background, a navigation app, or a game can keep the processor working hard.

3

Update iOS and restart

After a big iOS update it’s normal for the phone to run warm for a day or two while it re-indexes. If it stays hot after that, restart and check for app updates too.

4

Use an Apple or certified charger

Worn, frayed, or cheap cables and adapters are a common cause of hot charging. Try a different cable and adapter that you trust.

Software Heat vs Hardware Heat

Usually software or usage

Warm during heavy use, after an update, or while charging and streaming at the same time, and it cools down once you stop.

Often the battery

Gets hot with light use, drains fast, shuts off at higher percentages, or Battery Health shows “Service.” An aging battery works harder and runs warmer.

Possibly the board or port

Hot in one spot even when idle, hot after a drop or spill, or hot only when plugged in. These are worth a professional look.

After water exposure

Moisture can cause a short that creates heat. Stop charging and get it checked; drying it in rice won’t fix corrosion.

iPhone Overheating FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is it normal for my iPhone to get warm while charging?

Some warmth is normal, especially with fast charging or if you’re using the phone while it charges. It shouldn’t get too hot to hold, and it should cool down once charging slows near full. If it’s hot every time, try a different cable and adapter, then have it checked.

Can a bad battery make an iPhone overheat?

Yes. An older or damaged battery can run hot, drain quickly, and cause unexpected shutdowns. You can check Settings › Battery › Battery Health. A swollen battery is a safety issue, so stop charging and bring it in.

Should I put my hot iPhone in the fridge or freezer?

No. Fast cooling can cause condensation inside the phone, which can lead to water damage. Remove the case, move it somewhere shaded and room temperature, and let it cool on its own.

My iPhone got hot after it got wet. What should I do?

Stop charging it, power it off if you can, and bring it in soon. Water can cause short circuits that create heat and corrosion that gets worse over time. Rice won’t fix that.
