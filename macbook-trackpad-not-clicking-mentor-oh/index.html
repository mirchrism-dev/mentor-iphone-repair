MacBook Trackpad Won’t Click, Click Softly, or Move the Cursor?

A MacBook trackpad that won’t click, clicks only in one corner, or stops moving the cursor can make the laptop almost unusable. On newer Force Touch trackpads the “click” is haptic, so a battery that’s nearly dead or a failing trackpad board can kill the click entirely. Common Causes

Low or failing battery

On many recent MacBooks the trackpad click needs battery power. A nearly dead or worn battery can make clicks disappear even while the laptop runs on the charger.

Debris or liquid under the trackpad

Crumbs, dust, or a spill can keep the trackpad from traveling, or corrode the board underneath.

A failing trackpad or cable

Age, a drop, or repeated flexing of a hinge area can damage the trackpad assembly or its ribbon cable.

Software or accessibility settings

Slow Keys, a stuck Bluetooth mouse conflict, or a system glitch can make the trackpad feel dead when the hardware is fine.

Quick Checks Before You Panic

1

Plug in and charge for 20 minutes

If the battery was nearly empty, give it a bit of charge, then try clicking again.

2

Check trackpad settings

Open System Settings, then Trackpad. Make sure tracking is on and try Force Click / click options for your model.

3

Disconnect other pointing devices

Turn off Bluetooth briefly or unplug a USB mouse to rule out conflicts.

4

Restart in Safe Mode

A Safe Mode boot can clear a software glitch. If the trackpad works there, we can dig into software next.

Battery swelling can also lift or disable a trackpad. See MacBook battery replacement and MacBook repair.

MacBook Trackpad FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why did my MacBook trackpad suddenly stop clicking?

A nearly dead battery is a frequent cause on Force Touch models. Debris, liquid, a failing trackpad board, or a settings/software glitch are also common.

My trackpad works but won’t click. Is that different?

Yes. Cursor movement can still work while the haptic click fails, especially with a low battery or a failing force sensor. That’s useful diagnostic info when you bring it in.

Can a swollen battery affect the trackpad?

Yes. A swollen battery can press against the trackpad from below, making clicks mushy or impossible. Don’t keep using a laptop with a clearly swollen battery.

Do I need a whole new top case?

Sometimes the trackpad alone can be replaced; on some models Apple-style service uses a larger top-case assembly. We’ll tell you what’s realistic for your year and model before any work starts.
