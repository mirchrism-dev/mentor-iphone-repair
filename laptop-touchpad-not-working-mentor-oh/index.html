Laptop Touchpad Not Working?

A dead touchpad is one of the most frustrating laptop problems, because you can’t do much without a cursor. The good news: a lot of the time it has simply been switched off by a key press or a setting. Try the steps below with a USB mouse or the keyboard. If it still won’t respond, or the touchpad looks raised or the case is bulging, bring it to Mentor iPhone and Computer at 7745 Mentor Ave, Mentor, OH 44060.

Check This First: Is the Touchpad Raised?

If the touchpad sits higher than it used to, won’t click, or the bottom of the laptop is starting to bulge, the battery underneath may be swelling. That’s a safety issue, not a settings problem. Stop charging it, don’t press on it, and bring it in. See laptop battery replacement.

Things to Try

1

Look for a touchpad key

Many laptops have a function key with a small touchpad icon (often F5, F6, F7, or F9, sometimes with the Fn key). Press it once and test again. Some HP laptops use a double-tap in the top-left corner of the touchpad instead.

2

Check the Windows touchpad setting

Press the Windows key, type “touchpad,” and open Touchpad settings. Make sure the Touchpad switch is on. Also check the option that turns the touchpad off when a mouse is connected.

3

Restart the laptop

A full restart clears a stuck driver more often than you’d think. Use the keyboard: Windows key, then Tab and the arrow keys to reach Power.

4

Update or reinstall the driver

Open Device Manager and look under “Mice and other pointing devices” and “Human Interface Devices.” Update the touchpad driver, or uninstall it and restart so Windows reinstalls it. Your laptop maker’s support site often has the newest driver.

Touchpad stopped right after a Windows update? A driver change is the most likely cause.

Narrow It Down

Cursor jumps around

Often dirt, lotion, or moisture on the pad, or a palm-rejection setting. Clean the surface with a slightly damp cloth and dry it.

Moves but won’t click

Try tapping instead of pressing. If physical clicks stopped, the button may be worn or something is pushing on it from below.

Works in the BIOS but not Windows

That points to a driver or software problem, which is usually fixable without parts.

Dead after a spill

Shut it down and don’t keep testing. See laptop water damage repair.

Using a MacBook?

MacBook trackpads have their own quirks. See MacBook trackpad not clicking. For other Windows problems, our Windows PC software support page covers drivers, updates, and slowdowns.

Laptop Touchpad FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why did my touchpad suddenly stop working?

The most common reasons are a function key that turned it off, a setting that disables it when a mouse is plugged in, or a driver change after a Windows update.

Is a raised touchpad dangerous?

It can be. A swollen battery can push the touchpad up from underneath. Stop using and charging the laptop and have it checked.

Can you replace a laptop touchpad?

It depends on the make, model, and parts availability. Call (440) 525-5705 with your laptop’s model and we’ll let you know.

Can I just use a mouse instead?

Sure, a USB or Bluetooth mouse works as a stopgap. But if the cause is a swelling battery, don’t keep using the laptop.
