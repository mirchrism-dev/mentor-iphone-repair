Quick Checks You Can Do at Home

1

Restart it, for real

Use Restart rather than closing the lid or shutting down with Fast Startup. A true restart clears stuck updates and processes that build up over days or weeks.

2

Trim startup programs

On Windows, open Task Manager and look at the Startup tab. On a Mac, check Login Items in System Settings. Turning off programs you don’t need at startup can make a noticeable difference.

3

Free up storage space

A drive that’s nearly full slows everything down. Empty the Downloads folder, uninstall programs you don’t use, and move old photos and videos to an external drive or cloud storage.

4

Install pending updates

Let Windows Update or macOS updates finish, then restart. Half-installed updates are a common cause of slow startups.

Be careful with “PC cleaner” or “speed booster” ads and pop-ups. Many are unwanted software themselves, and some are scams. If a pop-up gives you a phone number to call, don’t call it.

Common Reasons a Computer Slows Down

An older spinning hard drive

Many older laptops and desktops still use a mechanical hard drive. Moving to a solid-state drive (SSD) can make a big difference, and we can tell you whether it makes sense for your model.

Unwanted software or malware

Toolbars, fake antivirus programs, and malware use up memory and processor time. Our Windows PC software support covers cleanup and safe setup.

Not enough memory (RAM)

Lots of browser tabs plus modern apps can outgrow older machines. Some computers can take more memory, and some can’t. We’ll check yours.

Heat and dust

A clogged fan makes the processor slow itself down to stay cool. If it’s hot and loud, that’s a clue.

Slow Computer FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is my computer slow because of a virus?

Sometimes, but not always. Malware and unwanted programs can slow a computer down, and so can a full drive, an old hard drive, too little memory, or overheating. We check for all of them so you get the right fix.

Will an SSD upgrade make my computer faster?

If your computer still has a mechanical hard drive, an SSD upgrade is often the biggest single improvement you can make. Some laptops have storage built onto the board and can’t be upgraded, so we’ll check your model first.

Do I need to reset Windows to speed it up?

Not usually as a first step. Cleanup and fixing the specific cause often help without a reset. If a reset does make sense, we’ll talk with you about backing up your files first.

Is it worth fixing an older slow computer?

It depends on the computer’s age, condition, and what you use it for. We’ll give you an honest opinion and quote before doing any work.
