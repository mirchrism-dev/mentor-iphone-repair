External Hard Drive Not Showing Up?

External drives often hold the only copy of family photos or old work files, so it’s scary when one stops showing up. Sometimes the fix is as simple as a different cable. Other times the drive is failing, and the wrong move can make recovery harder. Safe Things to Try First

1

Try a different cable and port

Worn USB cables are a common cause. Plug straight into the computer, not a hub or keyboard port.

2

Check the power

Larger desktop drives need their own power adapter. Make sure it’s plugged in and the light comes on.

3

Try another computer

If it works on a different computer, the drive is probably fine and the problem is on the first computer.

4

Look in Disk Management or Disk Utility

On Windows, open Disk Management. On a Mac, open Disk Utility. If the drive shows up there without a letter or name, it may just need one assigned.

When to Stop and Get Help

Clicking, beeping, or grinding

These sounds usually mean a mechanical problem. Unplug it and stop trying, because running a failing drive can cause more damage.

It asks you to format the drive

Don’t click Format if you need the files. Formatting can make recovery much harder.

It was dropped

Drops are hard on spinning drives, even if they look fine on the outside.

Very slow or freezing the computer

A drive that takes forever to open or freezes File Explorer or Finder may be failing. Copy the most important files first if you still can.

External Drive Not Recognized FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why is my external hard drive not showing up?

Common causes are a bad cable or port, not enough power, a missing drive letter, or a drive that’s failing. Try a different cable and computer first.

My drive is clicking. Can the files be saved?

Sometimes, but a clicking drive often has a mechanical problem. Stop using it right away. Repeated tries can damage it further and make recovery harder or more expensive.

Windows says I need to format the disk. Should I?

Not if you need the files. Formatting erases the drive’s file list. Cancel the message and get the files copied off first.

Can the drive be fine but the case be broken?

Yes. Many external drives are a regular drive inside a small case with a USB board. If the board or connector fails, the drive inside may still work.
