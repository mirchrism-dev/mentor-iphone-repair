Lines, Flicker, or a Green Tint on Your iPhone Screen?

Sometimes the glass on an iPhone looks perfect, but the picture underneath doesn’t: a bright green line down the side, a screen that flickers, or a dark blot that spreads. These problems almost always come from the display panel itself or its connection, not from the glass on top. What the Symptom Usually Means

One or more thin lines

A single green, pink, or white line, often running top to bottom, usually means the display panel is damaged, sometimes from an old drop that didn’t crack the glass.

Flickering or flashing

Flicker can come from a loose or damaged display connection, a failing panel, or, less often, a software glitch after an update.

Black spots or spreading ink

Dark areas that grow over days mean the OLED layer is damaged. They tend to get bigger, not smaller.

Green or pink tint

A screen that looks washed in one color can be a panel problem, but check your display settings first, since some accessibility filters change the color.

Quick Checks Before You Bring It In

1

Restart the iPhone

A full restart clears a rare software glitch. If the lines are still there, a restart won’t fix hardware.

2

Take a screenshot

If the lines show up in the screenshot, it’s likely software. If the screenshot looks clean, the problem is in the screen itself.

3

Check display filters

In Settings, look under Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, and make sure Color Filters and similar options are off.

4

Update iOS

Install any waiting update in case the flicker is a known software issue.

5

Back it up now

If the screen is getting worse, back up to iCloud or a computer while you can still see and tap it.

Lines on iPhone Screen FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why does my iPhone have a green line on the screen?

A green line usually means the OLED display panel is damaged or its connection has been disturbed. It can show up right after a drop or weeks later. A screenshot test helps confirm it’s the screen.

Will the lines go away on their own?

Hardware lines rarely go away, and black spots tend to spread. If a restart and update don’t help, plan on a display repair.

Is it the screen or the iPhone itself?

Most of the time it’s the display. If the screenshot looks normal, the phone’s internals are usually fine. We can confirm before you pay for anything.

Will I lose my data getting the screen replaced?

A screen replacement doesn’t normally erase your data, but we always recommend a backup before any repair.
