Try These Steps First

1

Charge it for 30 minutes

Plug into a wall adapter with a cable you trust and leave it alone for a full half hour. A deeply drained battery can take a while before the screen shows anything.

2

Force restart it

On iPhone 8 and newer: press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears. This fixes a frozen phone without erasing anything.

3

Check whether it’s really off

Call the phone, flip the ring/silent switch, or plug it in and listen for a vibration. If it rings or buzzes but the screen stays black, the display is the likely problem—not the phone.

4

Stuck on the Apple logo?

A phone that loops on the Apple logo may need a software repair. Some recovery options can erase the phone, so if your photos aren’t backed up, stop and talk to us first.

Common Reasons an iPhone Won’t Turn On

Worn-out battery

An aging battery can drop to nothing without warning. A battery replacement often brings the phone right back.

Failed screen

After a drop, the screen can die while the phone still works. A screen repair fixes that.

Charging problems

If no power gets in, the phone can’t start. A dirty or worn charging port is a common cause.

Liquid or board damage

Spills and hard impacts can damage internal parts. We’ll be honest about whether a repair makes sense, and about data recovery options.

We won’t reset or erase your iPhone without asking. If your data matters most, tell us up front so we can plan around it.

Will I lose my photos if my iPhone won’t turn on?

Not necessarily. Many dead-looking iPhones come back after a battery, screen, or port repair with everything intact. We don’t reset phones without your OK, and if the data isn’t backed up, tell us so we can plan around it.

My iPhone vibrates but the screen stays black. What’s wrong?

That usually means the phone is running but the display has failed—common after a drop. A screen repair typically fixes it, often the same day when parts are ready.

Often, yes. It can be a software issue or a hardware problem like a failing battery. Some software fixes can erase the phone, so bring it in before trying a full restore if your photos aren’t backed up.
