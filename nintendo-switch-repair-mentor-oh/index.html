Try These Before You Bring It In

1

Charge it with the official adapter for an hour

A Switch with a fully drained battery can sit dark for a while before it shows anything. Use the Nintendo power adapter rather than a phone charger, and leave it alone for about an hour.

2

Do a hard restart

Hold the power button for about 12 seconds to force it off, then press it once to turn it back on. This clears a lot of “frozen black screen” cases.

3

Check the dock setup

If the TV shows nothing, plug the power adapter into the dock first, then the HDMI cable, then set the console in. Try a different HDMI input or cable to rule out the TV side.

4

Note what happened before it stopped

A drop, a spill, a cheap third-party charger or dock, or a recent update all point to different causes, so tell us when you call.

The Switch Lite has no dock output and its controls are built in, so stick or button problems on a Lite mean the whole console comes in.

Common Switch Problems We See

Won’t charge or turn on

A worn or damaged USB-C charging port, a battery that has run down too far, or a power fault on the board. We test which one it is before suggesting a part.

No picture on the TV

Usually the dock, the cable, or the console’s USB-C port not making a clean connection. Third-party docks are a frequent suspect.

Cracked or dead screen

The original Switch and the Lite use an LCD panel; the OLED model uses an OLED panel. A crack, lines, or a black screen with sound still playing are all things to have checked.

Game card not reading

Dust or a bent pin in the game card slot can stop cartridges from loading while downloaded games still work fine.

Nintendo Switch Repair FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Which Switch models can you look at?

Bring in the original Nintendo Switch, the Switch OLED, or the Switch Lite. If you have a different model or something unusual, call and ask first.

Should I bring the dock and charger too?

Yes, if the problem involves charging or the TV. Bringing the dock, the power adapter, and the HDMI cable lets us figure out whether the fault is in the console or the accessories.

Will I lose my games and saves?

A hardware repair usually doesn’t touch your data, but we can’t promise that for every repair. Saves for many games back up to Nintendo Switch Online if you subscribe, so it’s worth checking that before you drop it off.
