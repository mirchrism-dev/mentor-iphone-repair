Your iPhone keeps dropping your home Wi-Fi, the Wi-Fi switch is grayed out, or your AirPods and car stereo suddenly won’t pair. Try These First

1

Restart the iPhone and the router

Turn the phone off and back on. If only your home Wi-Fi is the problem, unplug the router for 30 seconds too. Check whether other devices connect; if nothing connects, the issue is the network, not the phone.

2

Forget the network or device

In Settings, tap the Wi-Fi network or Bluetooth device, choose Forget, and reconnect. This clears a bad saved password or a stale pairing.

3

Update iOS

Go to Settings, General, Software Update. Connection bugs are often fixed in updates.

4

Reset Network Settings

Settings, General, Transfer or Reset iPhone, Reset, Reset Network Settings. This doesn’t delete photos or apps, but you’ll need to re-enter Wi-Fi passwords and re-pair Bluetooth devices.

Signs It’s Probably Hardware

  • The Wi-Fi or Bluetooth switch is grayed out and won’t turn on
  • The phone can’t find any networks, even right next to a working router
  • Signal is very weak only on this phone while other phones work fine in the same spot
  • The problem started right after a drop, a repair, or getting the phone wet
  • Bluetooth shows no devices at all, even ones in pairing mode nearby

Honest note: when the wireless chip on the logic board itself has failed, that’s a board-level problem, and on some models it may not be worth repairing. We’ll tell you what we find and what it would realistically cost before any work starts, so you can decide whether to repair or replace.

iPhone Wi-Fi & Bluetooth FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why is Wi-Fi grayed out on my iPhone?

A grayed-out Wi-Fi switch usually points to hardware, often after a drop or liquid damage. Try a restart and an iOS update first. If it stays gray, bring it in for a diagnosis.

Will Reset Network Settings erase my photos?

No. It only clears saved Wi-Fi networks and passwords, Bluetooth pairings, and VPN and cellular settings. Your photos, messages, and apps stay put. Backing up first is still a good habit.

My iPhone connects to Wi-Fi everywhere except home. Is it the phone?

Usually not. If the phone works on other networks, the problem is more likely the router or its settings. Restart the router and forget and rejoin the network before assuming the phone is broken.

Can you fix Bluetooth that stopped working after a screen repair elsewhere?

Possibly. Sometimes an antenna cable or connector gets disturbed during a repair. We can open it up, check the connections, and tell you what we find.
