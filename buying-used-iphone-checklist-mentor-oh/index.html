Buying a Used iPhone? Check These Things Before You Pay

A used iPhone can be a great deal, or a locked phone you can’t use. Most problems are easy to spot if you know where to look, and it only takes about ten minutes with the phone in your hand. 1. Make Sure It Isn’t Locked

  • Have the seller erase the phone in front of you so it shows the Hello setup screen
  • Start setup. If it asks for someone else’s Apple Account password, it’s Activation Lock, and you shouldn’t buy it
  • Look up the model and IMEI in Settings, General, About, and ask the seller’s carrier status. A phone reported lost or stolen may not work on cellular
  • In Settings, General, About, check Carrier Lock. “No SIM restrictions” means it’s unlocked

Activation Lock can only be removed by the original owner or Apple with proof of purchase. No repair shop can legitimately bypass it, so walk away from a locked phone.

2. Check the Battery and Parts History

1

Battery health

Settings, Battery, Battery Health (or Battery Health & Charging). Under about 80% means you’ll likely want a battery soon; factor that into the price.

2

Parts and Service History

On newer models, Settings, General, About shows whether the screen, battery, or camera was replaced and whether the part is genuine or unknown. An unknown part isn’t always bad, but it should lower the price.

3

Look at the screen closely

Turn brightness up on a white screen and look for dead spots, lines, or tinted areas. Check the edges and back glass for cracks.

3. Test the Things You Use Every Day

Face ID or Touch ID

Set it up. If setup fails, that’s often a costly or unfixable repair.

Cameras

Open the camera, switch between every lens and the front camera, and check focus and flash.

Calls and sound

Make a call with your SIM if you can. Test the earpiece, speakerphone, and microphone with a voice memo.

Charging

Plug it in with your own cable and make sure it charges without wiggling.

Buttons and Wi-Fi

Press every button, flip the ring or Action button, and join a Wi-Fi network.

Buying a Used iPhone FAQs (Mentor, OH)

How do I know if a used iPhone is stolen?

There’s no perfect check, but red flags include Activation Lock, a seller who won’t erase it in front of you, no box or receipt with a story that doesn’t fit, and a price far below others. Meet in a public place and take your time.

Can you remove Activation Lock from a used iPhone?

No. Only the original owner, or Apple with proof of purchase, can remove it. If a used iPhone asks for someone else’s Apple Account, don’t buy it.

What battery health is good for a used iPhone?

Higher is better. Around 80% or lower usually means the battery is worn and a replacement is coming. That’s a fixable problem, so use it to negotiate.

Is a replaced screen a dealbreaker?

Not necessarily. A quality replacement works fine. Check that touch, brightness, and True Tone work, and treat an unknown part as a reason to pay less.
