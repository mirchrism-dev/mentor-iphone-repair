The Simple Rule: Two Copies, Two Places

  • Keep your important files on the computer and on at least one other device, such as an external drive
  • If you can, keep a third copy somewhere else, such as a cloud service, so a fire, theft, or spill doesn’t take everything
  • A single external drive that also holds the only copy of old files is storage, not a backup

Ways to Back Up

Mac: Time Machine

Built into every Mac. Plug in an external drive, choose it in Time Machine settings, and it backs up automatically while the drive is connected.

Windows: File History or Windows Backup

Windows includes tools to copy your Documents, Desktop, Pictures, and other folders to an external drive or to your Microsoft account’s OneDrive.

Cloud storage

OneDrive, iCloud Drive, and Google Drive keep copies of the folders you choose online. Check how much space your plan includes.

Manual copy

Dragging folders to an external drive works in a pinch. The catch is remembering to do it again.

Make Sure It Actually Works

1

Check what’s included

Photos, documents, desktop files, and anything saved in odd places, like a scanner or tax program folder.

2

Look at the last backup date

Time Machine and Windows both show when the last backup finished. Old dates mean it stopped running.

3

Try restoring one file

Open the backup and bring back a single test file. If that works, you know the backup is real.

4

Write down your passwords

Cloud backups need your account login to restore. Keep it somewhere safe that isn’t only on the computer.

Computer Backup FAQs (Mentor, OH)

How often should I back up my computer?

Automatic backups are best because you don’t have to remember. If you back up by hand, do it at least once a month, and right before any repair, upgrade, or move to a new computer.

Is cloud storage the same as a backup?

Not always. Some sync services delete a file everywhere when you delete it on one device. Check whether your service keeps deleted files or older versions.

What size external drive do I need?

Look at how much space you’re using on your computer and pick a drive a good bit bigger, so there’s room for new files and older versions.

Should I back up before a computer repair?

Yes, whenever you can. Most repairs don’t touch your files, but a backup means you’re covered either way.
