Usually OK to Wait a Little

  • A single hairline crack near an edge, with touch and display working normally
  • A cracked screen protector while the glass underneath is fine (peel it off and check)
  • Cracks you can’t feel with a fingertip and that aren’t spreading

Even then, a cheap screen protector over the crack keeps glass slivers off your fingers and slows the crack from spreading.

Get It Looked At Soon

Glass is chipping or sharp

Loose glass can cut you and keeps flaking off. Cover it with tape or a protector and bring it in.

Lines, black spots, or green tint

That’s the display underneath, not just the glass. These often spread, and the phone can go black without warning.

Ghost touches or dead zones

If the phone taps by itself or part of the screen won’t respond, the touch layer is damaged.

Screen lifting or bulging

A screen pushed up from inside can mean a swollen battery. Stop charging it and bring it in right away.

Cracks near the camera or Face ID

Damage at the top of an iPhone can affect the front camera and Face ID sensors.

Keep It From Getting Worse

1

Back up now

If the screen gets worse, a backup is the difference between a repair and lost photos. Use iCloud, Google, or a computer while the screen still works.

2

Keep it dry

Cracks mean the phone is no longer as water-resistant as it was. Keep it out of the rain and away from the sink.

3

Avoid pressure

Don’t keep it in a back pocket, and use a case with a raised edge so the screen doesn’t take the next drop.

Cracked Phone Screen FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Can a cracked screen hurt my phone?

It can. Cracks let in moisture and dust, and the damage often spreads to the display or touch layer. A small crack is a cheaper problem than a dead display.

Is it dangerous to use a phone with a cracked screen?

Mostly the risk is small glass cuts. A screen that’s lifting is different, because that can mean a swollen battery, so stop charging and get it checked.

Will the crack spread?

Often, yes. Temperature changes, pressure in a pocket, and small bumps all help cracks grow. A protector over the crack can slow it down.
