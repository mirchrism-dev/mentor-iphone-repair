A scary pop-up, a weird text, or a battery that suddenly drains fast can make anyone wonder if their phone has been hacked. Signs Worth Taking Seriously

  • Password reset emails or login alerts you didn’t ask for
  • Friends getting texts or messages from you that you didn’t send
  • Apps you don’t remember installing, especially ones that ask to manage the device
  • On Android, an app that won’t uninstall or keeps showing full-screen ads
  • Charges on your carrier bill or app store account you don’t recognize

Common False Alarms

Pop-ups saying you have a virus

These are almost always ads in a web browser trying to scare you into calling or installing something. Close the tab and don’t call the number.

Battery draining faster

Usually an older battery, a new update settling in, or one app using a lot of power. Check battery usage in Settings.

Phone running warm

Charging, navigation, video, and weak signal all heat a phone up. Heat alone isn’t a sign of hacking.

Calendar spam on iPhone

Junk calendar events usually come from accidentally subscribing to a calendar. Delete the subscription in Settings.

What to Do Right Now

1

Secure your main account

Change your Apple Account or Google password from a device you trust, and turn on two-factor authentication.

2

Check signed-in devices

In your Apple Account or Google account settings, look at the list of devices signed in and remove any you don’t recognize.

3

Change email and banking passwords

Your email is the key to everything else. Use a different password for each important account.

4

Update the phone and remove odd apps

Install the latest software update and delete apps you don’t recognize or no longer use.

5

Call your carrier about strange texts or a lost signal

If your phone suddenly loses service and you get account alerts, contact your carrier right away. Someone may be trying to move your number.

Is My Phone Hacked? FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Can someone hack my iPhone just by texting me?

It’s extremely rare for regular people. The bigger risk is tapping a link in a scam text and typing your password into a fake page. Keep your iPhone updated and don’t sign in from links in texts.

Do iPhones get viruses?

Traditional viruses on iPhone are very rare. Problems usually come from scam websites, junk calendar subscriptions, or a stolen Apple Account password.

Should I factory reset my phone if I think it’s hacked?

Secure your accounts first, because a reset doesn’t fix a stolen password. A reset can help if you have a bad app you can’t remove, but back up first.

I called the number on a virus pop-up. What now?

Don’t give out any more information. Change your passwords, call your bank if you shared card details, and if you installed anything they asked for, have the device checked.
