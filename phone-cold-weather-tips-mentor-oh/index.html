Why Cold Shuts Phones Off

Batteries slow down when cold

Lithium-ion batteries can’t deliver power as easily below freezing. The phone sees a voltage drop and shuts off to protect itself, even with charge left.

Worn batteries get hit harder

A healthy battery may just drain faster in the cold. An older battery is more likely to shut the phone off completely.

It usually comes back

Once the phone warms up, the charge often reappears. Permanent damage is less common, but the cold exposes a weak battery.

Winter Habits That Help

  • Keep your phone in an inside pocket, close to your body, instead of a coat’s outer pocket or a cup holder
  • Don’t leave it in the car overnight; a car in a Mentor January gets far colder than a phone is designed for
  • Let a cold phone warm up to room temperature before charging it
  • Don’t warm it on a heater, vent, or in a microwave, and don’t use a hair dryer on it
  • If it came in from the cold and fogged up or got snow in the ports, let it dry before plugging it in
  • Use a case with a decent grip; a drop on ice is a common way screens crack in winter

Phones in Cold Weather FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is it bad for my phone to be in the cold?

Short trips are fine. Long periods below freezing drain the battery fast and can make the phone shut off. Charging while it’s still very cold is what you most want to avoid.

My phone shut off outside but has charge now. Is it broken?

Probably not. That’s typical cold-weather behavior. If it starts shutting off indoors too, the battery is likely worn and worth getting checked.

Should I charge my phone right after coming inside?

Give it a little time to reach room temperature first, especially if it fogged up or got snow in the port. Then charge as usual.

Does a new battery help in winter?

Yes, if your current one is worn. A fresh battery handles cold much better than an old one, though any phone drains faster in freezing temperatures.
