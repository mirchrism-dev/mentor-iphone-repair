If It’s a Hand-Me-Down Phone

1

Back up and erase it

Back up your own photos and messages, sign out of your Apple Account or Google account, then erase the phone. Don’t just delete apps, because your accounts stay attached.

2

Check the battery

Old phones often have worn batteries. On iPhone, look at Battery Health in Settings. A phone that dies by lunch is a frustrating first phone.

3

Check the screen and charging port

Hairline cracks and a loose charging port get worse with kid use. It’s easier to fix them before the phone changes hands.

Need those fixed first? See iPhone battery replacement, iPhone screen repair, or charging port repair.

Set Up Their Account the Right Way

iPhone: Family Sharing

Create a child Apple Account through Family Sharing instead of letting them use yours. That lets you use Screen Time and Ask to Buy.

Android: Google Family Link

Set up the child’s Google account with Family Link to manage apps, screen time, and location.

Approve purchases

Turn on Ask to Buy or purchase approvals so in-app purchases don’t land on your card.

Location sharing

Turn on location sharing with your family, and make sure Find My (iPhone) or Find My Device (Android) is on in case the phone gets lost.

Protect the Phone Itself

  • A case with raised edges and a screen protector, put on before the first day of use
  • Set a passcode you know, and teach them not to share it with friends
  • Write down the Apple Account or Google password somewhere safe; you’ll need it to reset or repair the phone later
  • Turn on automatic backups so photos aren’t lost if the phone is

Kid’s First Phone FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Should my kid’s first phone be new or a hand-me-down?

A hand-me-down is often fine if it still gets software updates and the battery is healthy. A fresh battery can make an older iPhone feel like a much better phone for a lot less than a new one.

How do I stop my child from buying apps on my card?

Use a child account through Family Sharing (iPhone) or Family Link (Android) and turn on purchase approvals, so every purchase needs your OK.

Can I see where my child’s phone is?

Yes. Family location sharing on iPhone and Family Link on Android let you see the phone’s location, as long as it’s turned on and the phone has power.

What’s the best way to keep a kid’s phone from breaking?

A good case with raised edges plus a glass screen protector prevents most damage from everyday drops. Nothing is drop-proof, so keep backups on.
