Common Scams Right Now

Unpaid toll or fine

A text says you owe a small toll or ticket and links to a payment page. Real toll agencies don’t usually collect by random text link.

Package delivery problem

“Your package is on hold, confirm your address.” The link leads to a fake page that asks for card details.

Bank or card alert

A text or call about a locked account or suspicious charge that pushes you to act now or read back a code.

“Tech support” calls and pop-ups

Someone claiming to be Apple, Microsoft, or Amazon says your device is hacked and wants remote access or gift cards. See computer scam pop-up help.

Wrong number or “Hi Mom”

A friendly message from a stranger, or someone pretending to be family with a new number who needs money fast.

Red Flags

  • Urgency: pay today, act in 24 hours, or your account closes
  • A link with a strange or misspelled web address
  • Requests for gift cards, wire transfers, crypto, or payment apps
  • Anyone asking you to read back a verification code sent to your phone
  • A caller who wants to install an app or connect to your device remotely

If You Already Tapped the Link or Answered

1

Stop and don’t enter anything else

If you didn’t type in passwords or card numbers, you’re usually in good shape. Close the page.

2

Call your bank if you paid or shared card info

Use the number on the back of your card, not one from the text.

3

Change passwords you typed in

Start with email and banking, and turn on two-factor authentication where you can.

4

Remove anything you were told to install

Delete unfamiliar apps or remote-access tools. On Android, a suspicious app can keep showing ads or pop-ups; see Android virus removal.

Forward scam texts to 7726 (SPAM) to report them to your carrier, then delete them.

Cut Down on Scam Messages

On iPhone, you can filter messages from unknown senders in Settings, under Apps, Messages (or Messages on older iOS). Many Android phones have caller ID and spam protection in the Phone app settings. If you’re worried your phone is already compromised, read signs your phone is hacked.

Scam Texts & Calls FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Can I get hacked just by opening a scam text?

Opening the message itself is very unlikely to harm a phone that’s up to date. The risk comes from tapping the link and entering information, or installing something you’re asked to install.

Should I reply STOP to a scam text?

No. Replying tells scammers the number is active. Report it and delete it instead.

How do I know if a text is really from my bank?

Don’t use the link or number in the text. Open your bank’s app or call the number on your card to check.
