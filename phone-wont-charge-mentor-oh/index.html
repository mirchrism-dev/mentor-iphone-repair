Quick Checks to Try First

1

Swap the cable and the adapter

Cables fray inside long before they look bad. Try a different cable and wall adapter you know works, and a different outlet. If the phone charges now, you’ve saved yourself a trip.

2

Look in the port with a flashlight

Pocket lint packs into charging ports and stops the plug from seating fully. Power the phone off first and never dig with metal like a pin or paper clip—that can short or bend the contacts. If you see packed debris, we can clean it safely.

3

Restart and check for warnings

Restart the phone and watch for messages like “liquid detected” or “accessory not supported.” A liquid warning means unplug and let it dry; don’t keep forcing a charge.

4

Try wireless charging if you have it

If the phone charges on a wireless pad but not with a cable, the charging port is the likely problem. If it won’t charge either way, the battery or internal charging circuit is more likely.

When It Needs a Repair

  • The plug feels loose, falls out, or only works at one angle — usually a worn or damaged charging port
  • It charges, but drops fast or shuts off at 20–30% — usually a worn-out battery
  • It stopped charging after a spill, rain, or a drop in the sink — possible liquid damage
  • It gets hot while charging or shows no sign of power at all — needs a proper diagnosis
  • Debris is packed so deep you can’t safely get it out

Phone Won’t Charge FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Why does my phone only charge when the cable is at an angle?

That usually means lint is packed into the port or the port’s contacts are worn. A careful cleaning sometimes fixes it; if the port is worn, we can replace it—often the same day.

How do I know if it’s the battery or the charging port?

If the phone charges on a wireless pad or at an odd angle, the port is the usual suspect. If it charges normally but dies quickly, it’s more likely the battery. We’ll test both in-store and tell you what we find before quoting.

Can I clean my charging port myself?

Power the phone off and use only something soft and non-metal, gently. Never use pins, paper clips, or liquids. If debris is packed in tight, bring it in and we’ll check and clean it for you.

My phone says liquid was detected. What should I do?

Unplug it, don’t keep trying to charge, and let it dry. If the warning won’t clear or the phone got properly wet, bring it in soon—quicker attention gives the best chance of a good outcome.
