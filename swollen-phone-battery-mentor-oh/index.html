Swollen Phone Battery? Don’t Keep Charging It

If your phone’s screen is lifting away from the frame, the back is bulging, or the phone suddenly rocks when you set it on a table, the battery inside has probably swollen. Lithium batteries can puff up as they age or after damage, and a swollen battery shouldn’t be ignored. It can crack the screen from the inside, and in rare cases it can overheat. Signs Your Battery Is Swelling

Screen lifting or a gap

A visible gap along one edge of the screen, or the display pushed up so it sits higher than the frame.

Back glass or case bulging

The back looks rounded, cracks for no reason, or a case that used to fit now feels tight.

Phone won’t sit flat

The phone spins or rocks when you tap it on a table. That’s a classic early sign.

Touch or display problems

Pressure from the battery can cause odd touch behavior, pressure marks, or lines on the screen.

What to Do Right Now

1

Stop charging it

Unplug it and don’t leave it charging overnight or on a bed or couch.

2

Don’t press or puncture it

Don’t try to push the screen back down, and never poke or cut into a swollen battery. Punctured lithium batteries can catch fire.

3

Back up if it still turns on

If the phone works, back up your photos and contacts now. Our phone backup guide shows how on iPhone and Android.

4

Keep it cool and bring it in

Store it somewhere cool and away from anything flammable, and get the battery replaced soon. If it’s hot, smoking, or hissing, move it away from people and anything that can burn, outdoors if you can do so safely, and call 911 if there’s a fire.

Swelling also happens in tablets and laptops. A laptop battery that swells can push up the keyboard or trackpad; see laptop battery replacement.

Swollen Battery FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is a swollen phone battery dangerous?

It can be. A swollen battery has been damaged inside, and it can crack the screen, overheat, or, rarely, catch fire, especially if it’s punctured. Stop charging it and get it replaced.

Why did my phone battery swell?

Most often it’s age and many charge cycles. Heat, physical damage, and poor-quality chargers can make it more likely.

Can I keep using my phone until I have time?

It’s best not to. Don’t charge it overnight, don’t press on it, and get it in as soon as you can.

Will the screen go back to normal after a new battery?

Often the screen sits flat again once the swollen battery is removed. If the swelling cracked or bent the screen, it may also need replacing. We’ll tell you before doing any extra work.
