Free Up Space Safely

1

Back up first

Before you delete anything, make sure your photos and data are backed up to iCloud or to a computer. If you aren’t sure whether they are, don’t delete photos yet.

2

Check what’s using the space

Go to Settings, then General, then iPhone Storage. The bar at the top shows what’s taking up room, and the list below sorts apps by size along with Apple’s suggestions.

3

Offload apps you rarely use

Offloading removes the app but keeps its documents and data, so you can reinstall it later and pick up where you left off. You can also turn on Offload Unused Apps on the same screen.

4

Clear the big hidden piles

Old message attachments and videos, downloaded shows and podcasts, and the Recently Deleted photo album (it holds deleted photos for 30 days) are common space hogs.

Turning on iCloud Photos with Optimize iPhone Storage keeps full-size originals in iCloud and smaller versions on the phone, but it needs enough iCloud space to hold your library.

Why iPhones Fill Up

Photos and video

4K video and years of photos are the biggest users of space on most iPhones we see.

Messages

Texts with photos, videos, and voice messages pile up quietly, especially in busy group chats.

System Data

The “System Data” or “Other” section includes caches and logs. It usually shrinks on its own, and a restart or update can help.

Big apps and downloads

Games, streaming downloads, and offline maps can each take several gigabytes.

iPhone Storage FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Will deleting photos from my iPhone delete them from iCloud?

If iCloud Photos is turned on, yes. Deleting a photo on the phone deletes it everywhere that library syncs. Copy them somewhere else first if you want to keep them.

What’s the difference between offloading and deleting an app?

Offloading removes the app but keeps its data, so reinstalling brings you back to where you were. Deleting removes the app and its data from the phone.

Why is System Data so large?

It holds caches, logs, and temporary files. It often goes down on its own over time, and a restart or iOS update can help. If it stays huge, we can take a look.

Can you add more storage to my iPhone?

No. iPhone storage is built into the phone and can’t be upgraded. Freeing space or moving photos to iCloud or a computer is the realistic fix.
