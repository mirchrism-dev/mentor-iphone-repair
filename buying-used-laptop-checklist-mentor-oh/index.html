Buying a Used Laptop? Check These First

A used laptop can be a good deal, especially for school or a second computer at home. Before You Meet the Seller

  • Ask for the exact model number so you can look up its age, specs, and whether it still gets updates
  • For Windows laptops, make sure it can run a supported version of Windows. See Windows 10 end of support
  • Ask if it has ever been dropped, spilled on, or repaired
  • Meet somewhere with power and Wi-Fi so you can actually test it

Checklist to Run in Person

1

Start it from fully off

Watch how long it takes to boot. Very slow startups can mean an old hard drive, which an SSD upgrade can often fix.

2

Check the battery

Unplug it and use it for a few minutes. On a Mac, check battery condition in System Settings. On Windows, a battery report can show how much capacity is left.

3

Look over the screen

Open a white page and a dark page. Look for dead spots, lines, flickering, or pressure marks.

4

Test keyboard, trackpad, and ports

Type in every key row, click the trackpad, plug in a USB device, and try the charger and headphone jack.

5

Open and close the lid

The hinge should feel firm and even. Creaking or a wobbly screen is worth noting. See laptop hinge repair.

6

Confirm it’s signed out and reset

A Mac should be signed out of Find My. A Windows laptop shouldn’t ask for someone else’s password or Microsoft account after a reset.

Red Flags

Seller won’t let you power it on

If you can’t test it, you can’t know what you’re buying.

Activation lock or someone else’s login

An account-locked laptop may not be usable no matter what you do. Walk away.

Swollen battery

If the case bulges or the trackpad sits raised, the battery may be swollen and needs attention. See laptop battery replacement.

Sticky keys or corrosion smell

Can be a sign of past liquid damage, which can cause problems later.

Buying a Used Laptop FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is a used laptop worth it?

It can be, if it’s recent enough to get updates and the battery, screen, and storage check out. Older models can cost more to fix than they’re worth. See repair or replace.

What’s the most common problem with used laptops?

Tired batteries and slow hard drives are the two we see most. Both are often fixable.

How do I know a MacBook isn’t locked?

Ask the seller to sign out of their Apple Account and turn off Find My in front of you, then erase the Mac. If it asks for their password at setup, don’t buy it.
