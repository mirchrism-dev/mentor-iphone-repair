Locked Out of Your Apple ID or Google Account?

Your Apple ID (now called your Apple Account) or Google account holds your photos, contacts, and purchases, and it’s also what locks your phone if it’s reset or stolen. Getting locked out is stressful, but the official recovery steps work for most people if you’re patient. This guide covers those steps, how to avoid people who promise to “unlock” accounts for a fee, and what a repair shop like Mentor iPhone and Computer at 7745 Mentor Ave, Mentor, OH 44060 honestly can and can’t do.

Start With the Official Recovery Steps

1

Apple: use a trusted device first

On an iPhone, iPad, or Mac you’re still signed in to, go to Settings, tap your name, then Sign-In & Security, and change the password. No device? Go to iforgot.apple.com or use the Apple Support app on a family member’s phone.

2

Apple: be patient with account recovery

If Apple can’t verify you right away, it may start account recovery, which can take days. Apple sends updates to your phone number. Using other devices signed into the account can cancel the wait, so leave them alone.

3

Google: use the recovery page

Go to accounts.google.com and choose “Forgot password?”. Use a device and location you’ve signed in from before, and answer with your best guesses when you’re not sure.

4

Check your recovery phone and email

Codes go to the recovery phone number and email on file. If you’ve changed numbers, try the old SIM or email address if you still have it.

Set Up Recovery Before You Need It

Recovery contact (Apple)

Pick someone you trust in Sign-In & Security. They can get you a code to help you back in. They can’t see your data.

Recovery key (Apple)

A recovery key adds security, but if you set one and lose it along with your devices, Apple can’t help you in. Store it somewhere safe and offline.

Backup codes (Google)

Print Google’s backup codes and keep a current recovery phone and email in your account settings.

Write it down

Keep your account email and password in a safe place at home or in a password manager. Our holiday gift guide recommends this for every new phone.

Avoid Scam “Recovery Services”

  • Apple and Google don’t charge to recover an account, and their staff won’t call or text you out of the blue asking for a code.
  • Never share a verification code with anyone. A code someone asks you to read back is usually how they take over the account.
  • Websites and social media accounts promising to unlock any account for a fee are almost always scams, and some are run by the same people who stole the account.
  • Got a suspicious text or call? See our scam texts and calls guide, and if you think someone got in, read signs your phone was hacked.

What a Repair Shop Can and Can’t Do

Here’s the honest answer: no legitimate shop can bypass Activation Lock on an iPhone or Factory Reset Protection (FRP) on an Android phone. Those locks exist to stop thieves, and only the account owner, Apple, or Google can remove them. If you own the phone but lost the account, Apple Support can review an Activation Lock request when you have proof of purchase. If you forgot your iPhone’s screen passcode, that’s a different problem; read iPhone forgot passcode. What we can do is repair the phone itself, and help with things like moving data to a new phone once you’re back in.

Account Recovery FAQs (Mentor, OH)

How long does Apple account recovery take?

It can take a few days or longer, depending on what information Apple can verify. Apple sends updates to your phone number, and using other signed-in devices can restart the wait.

Can I recover a Google account without my phone number?

Sometimes. Use the recovery page from a device and location you’ve used before, and try your recovery email. Google decides based on the information you can verify.

Can you unlock an iPhone with Activation Lock?

No. No legitimate repair shop can. Only the account owner, or Apple with proof of purchase, can remove Activation Lock.

Is a paid account recovery service legit?

Be very careful. Apple and Google don’t charge for recovery, and paid “unlock” services are often scams that take your money or your account.
