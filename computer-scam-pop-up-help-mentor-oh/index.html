Scam Pop-Up on Your Computer? Help in Mentor, OH

A loud warning fills the screen saying your computer is infected, your files are at risk, and you need to call “Microsoft Support” or “Apple Support” right away. Real Microsoft and Apple warnings don’t ask you to call a phone number in a pop-up. Those screens are scams designed to get you on the phone so someone can take control of your computer or ask for payment. If it happened to you, you’re not alone, and it’s usually fixable. What to Do Right Now

1

Don’t call the number

Don’t call the number on the screen, click the pop-up, or type anything into it.

2

Close the browser

Press Ctrl+Alt+Delete and open Task Manager to end the browser on Windows, or press Command+Option+Esc to force quit it on a Mac. If nothing responds, hold the power button to shut the computer down.

3

Don’t restore the last session

When you open the browser again, don’t click “restore pages.” That can reopen the same scam page.

4

If you already called or let them in

Disconnect the computer from Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable, call your bank or card company if you paid or shared account details, and change important passwords from a different, trusted device. Then have the computer checked.

If the pop-up comes back on its own, or someone had remote access, don’t keep using the computer for banking or email until it’s been looked at.

Pop-Up Only, or Something More?

Just a browser pop-up

Most scam screens are web pages, not real infections. They’re built to look frozen and frightening. Once the browser is closed and its history and notifications are cleared, many are gone.

Unwanted software or extensions

If pop-ups keep returning, a browser extension, notification permission, or installed program may be causing them, and it needs to be removed.

Remote access was granted

If you let a caller connect, they may have installed remote-access software or changed settings. That’s worth a careful check, not just a restart.

Scam Pop-Up FAQs (Mentor, OH)

Is a pop-up saying my computer is infected real?

A pop-up in your web browser that tells you to call a phone number is almost always a scam. Microsoft and Apple don’t put support phone numbers in warning pop-ups or contact you that way.

I called the number and gave them access. What now?

Disconnect the computer from the internet, contact your bank or card company if you paid or shared financial details, and change important passwords from a different device. Then bring the computer in so we can check for anything left behind.

Will I lose my pictures and documents?

Usually not. In most cases a scam pop-up doesn’t touch your files. We’ll check the computer and talk with you before doing anything that could affect your data.

Can you help with a Mac too?

Yes. Scam pop-ups show up on Macs and Windows PCs alike. Bring in either one.
